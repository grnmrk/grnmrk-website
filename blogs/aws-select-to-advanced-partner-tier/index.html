How to Advance from AWS Select to Advanced Partner Tier

Most partners hit Select and stall for 18 months or more. The gap between Select and Advanced isn't just about checking more boxes — it's about understanding what AWS is actually evaluating and building a program that signals co-sell readiness. Here's exactly how to close that gap.

Home → Blog → AWS Select to Advanced

Why Most Partners Stall at Select

Getting to AWS Select is the easy part. AWS designed it that way. The requirements are achievable, the documentation is clear, and AWS actively wants partners to reach Select because it signals a baseline level of commitment to the ecosystem.

Advanced is a different conversation entirely. The partners who stall — and most do, often for 12 to 24 months — aren't failing because they lack the technology or the customers. They're stalling because they're treating the path to Advanced the same way they treated Select: as a checklist exercise.

It isn't. Advanced tier is AWS signaling that it trusts you as a co-sell partner. Every requirement is designed to answer one question: is this partner ready to show up in front of our customers and represent us?

The partners who advance fastest aren't necessarily the ones with the most certifications. They're the ones whose PDM is actively vouching for them internally — and that relationship is built deliberately, not passively.

The Advanced Tier Requirements, Explained

The AWS Partner Network updates its program requirements periodically, so always verify the current thresholds in your Partner Central portal. That said, the core categories have been stable for years and are unlikely to change significantly. Here's what you're working toward:

Requirement Area What AWS Looks For Common Gap
AWS Certifications Minimum number of active, validated certifications across your team Certifications lapse or are held by people who've left the company
Customer References Validated customer success stories showing real AWS deployments Partners have customers but haven't submitted them through the formal validation process
Annual Revenue AWS-attributed revenue above the Advanced threshold Revenue exists but isn't being properly attributed through ACE or the Partner Portal
ACE Pipeline Activity Active, healthy opportunity pipeline with consistent submission cadence ACE is treated as an afterthought — submissions are sporadic and opportunities are stale
Training Completion Partner-level training paths completed by your team Training is started but not completed, or not logged to the correct PO
From the Field

The single most common reason we see partners fail to advance isn't a hard requirement — it's revenue attribution. They have the customers, they have the AWS usage, but none of it is flowing through ACE in a way that AWS can credit to their partner account. Fix your attribution before you do anything else.

The Four Gaps That Actually Block Advancement

After working through this process with dozens of partners, the blockers almost always come back to four things. None of them are technical. All of them are operational.

Gap 1: Invisible Revenue

You have customers using AWS. Your products run on AWS. But if that activity isn't being submitted through ACE — with the opportunity properly linked to your AWS partner account and ideally co-owned with a field rep — AWS can't credit it to you. This is the most fixable and most overlooked gap in the program. Start here.

Gap 2: Stale or Thin Customer References

AWS requires validated customer references, not just customer names. The validation process involves AWS reaching out to confirm the engagement. Many partners have satisfied customers who haven't been submitted, or they've submitted customers whose engagements are too thin — a proof-of-concept that never went to production, for example. AWS wants to see substantive, ongoing AWS usage.

Gap 3: Certification Drift

AWS certifications expire. Team members leave. New hires join with different credential profiles. What was a clean certification count six months ago can silently fall below the Advanced threshold without anyone noticing. You need a living tracker — not a one-time count — that monitors expiration dates and maps credentials to headcount.

Gap 4: A PDM Who Doesn't Know You

This one isn't in the requirements document, but it matters more than most partners realize. Your Partner Development Manager reviews and approves tier advancement. If your PDM doesn't have context on who you are, what you're building with AWS, and why you deserve Advanced — your application goes in cold. Partners who advance quickly have almost always been in regular contact with their PDM for at least two quarters before applying.

Building the PDM Relationship That Accelerates Advancement

Your PDM is the single most important person in your AWS partner journey, and most partners treat them like a support ticket queue.

PDMs are overextended. A typical PDM manages 50 to 150 partners. They can't proactively support everyone — they triage. The partners who get attention are the ones who make it easy: clear updates, active ACE pipelines, concrete asks, and a demonstrated understanding of how the co-sell motion works.

Here's what a good PDM engagement cadence looks like:

  • Monthly email update — one paragraph on pipeline activity, one on any certifications or training completed, one ask or question. Keep it under 200 words.
  • Quarterly call — 30 minutes, agenda sent in advance. Cover your ACE pipeline, any funding you've applied for, and your tier advancement timeline. Come with specific questions.
  • Event touchpoints — if you're attending re:Invent or a regional AWS Summit, let your PDM know and try to get a face-to-face meeting. These carry disproportionate weight.

Don't wait until you're applying for Advanced to start building the PDM relationship. By then it's too late. The relationship needs to be warm before the application goes in.

Getting Your ACE Pipeline Right

ACE — the AWS Customer Engagements tool — is how AWS tracks your co-sell activity, validates your revenue attribution, and evaluates whether you're a partner worth investing in. It's also the thing most partners manage worst.

A healthy ACE pipeline for Advanced tier consideration means:

  • Active opportunities being submitted regularly — not in batches at quarter end
  • Opportunities with accurate stage, close date, ARR, and customer detail
  • A meaningful percentage of opportunities that are co-owned with AWS (meaning a field rep has accepted the opportunity)
  • Opportunities that progress through stages rather than sitting stale at the same status for months
  • Win/loss data being captured — AWS uses close rates to evaluate co-sell quality
Common Mistake

Partners often submit a batch of opportunities right before applying for Advanced tier. AWS can see your submission history. A sudden spike after months of inactivity doesn't look like program maturity — it looks like you're gaming the system. Build the habit over time.

The 90-Day Advancement Plan

If you're at Select and want to reach Advanced, here's the sequencing that works. This assumes you have a reasonable foundation — some certifications, some AWS customers, some ACE activity — and need to close the gaps systematically.

01

Days 1–14: Audit Your Current Standing

Pull your current partner scorecard from Partner Central. Document your exact position on every Advanced requirement: certifications (count, holders, expiration dates), customer references (submitted vs. eligible), revenue attribution, ACE pipeline health, and training completion. Don't guess — get the actual numbers.

02

Days 14–30: Fix Revenue Attribution

Identify every active customer where AWS is in the stack. For each one, check whether there's an ACE opportunity that captures that revenue. If not, create it. Work with your sales team to back-populate existing wins that should be in ACE but aren't. This alone often moves the needle significantly on your attributed revenue count.

03

Days 30–45: Submit Customer References

Identify your three strongest AWS customer engagements — production workloads, meaningful ARR, willing to be contacted by AWS. Submit them through the customer reference process in Partner Central. Don't wait for this — it takes time for references to be validated, and you want them cleared before you apply.

04

Days 45–60: Close Certification Gaps

Based on your audit, identify which certifications you need and who on your team is best positioned to get them. Prioritize certifications that serve double duty — ones that count toward Advanced requirements and also toward any specializations you're targeting. Schedule exams, not study sessions.

05

Days 60–75: Engage Your PDM

Schedule a call with your PDM and share your advancement timeline. Don't ask permission — tell them what you're doing and ask for their input on anything that might be holding you back from their perspective. Ask directly: "Is there anything you'd want to see before we apply for Advanced?" This conversation surfaces issues you can't see from the portal.

06

Days 75–90: Apply and Follow Up

Once your scorecard is clean, submit your application through Partner Central. Notify your PDM that the application is in. Follow up within two weeks if you haven't heard back. If you're rejected, ask your PDM specifically what needs to change — the feedback is almost always actionable.

What Changes When You Reach Advanced

Reaching Advanced tier isn't just a badge. It changes what's available to you inside the AWS ecosystem in material ways:

  • PDM access improves. Advanced partners typically get more PDM attention and a more senior contact. The relationship becomes more strategic.
  • Funding eligibility expands. Many MAP, MDF, and co-investment programs have higher funding limits or exclusive availability for Advanced and Premier partners.
  • Co-sell priority increases. AWS field reps are more willing to bring Advanced partners into deals. The tier signals credibility to people who don't know you.
  • Marketplace and Specialization paths open up. Some Marketplace programs and AWS Competencies require Advanced tier as a baseline.
  • Competitive differentiation sharpens. When a prospect is evaluating two partners, the one with Advanced tier has an edge — it's visible, it's third-party validated, and it signals investment in the AWS relationship.

Advanced tier is a platform, not a destination. The partners who get the most out of it are the ones who immediately start working toward the next thing — a Specialization, a Marketplace motion, or Premier tier — rather than treating it as the finish line.

The Bottom Line

Advancing from Select to Advanced isn't complicated, but it requires discipline and it requires someone actively managing the process. The partners who stall aren't missing something exotic — they're missing consistent execution on the fundamentals: clean ACE pipeline, validated customer references, current certifications, and an engaged PDM relationship.
