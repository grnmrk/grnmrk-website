The Situation
Two Failed Attempts. One Expensive Year.

This Series B security ISV had been trying to pass their AWS Foundational Technical Review for nearly 12 months. They attempted the review twice: once with internal resources and once with a generalist AWS consulting firm, and received remediation requests both times that stalled the process for months.

The core problem was not their product. Their architecture was solid and their security posture was genuinely strong. The failures were documentation failures. They had selected the wrong checklist track (submitting under SaaS requirements when their delivery model had AMI components), submitted incomplete evidence packages, and had not staged their ACE pipeline to meet the 10-opportunity prerequisite before submission.

Meanwhile, their PDM had cooled on them. Two failed FTR attempts signals low investment to the AWS field, even when it is not warranted.

What We Did
  • Audited both prior submission attempts to identify exactly where the reviews failed and why
  • Reclassified their delivery model correctly as hybrid SaaS/AMI and selected the appropriate combined checklist
  • Ran a full gap analysis against the correct checklist, prioritizing the 4 items that had triggered remediation requests in prior reviews
  • Rebuilt the ACE pipeline in parallel: staged 13 qualified opportunities across their top AWS territories to clear the prerequisite
  • Assembled a complete evidence package including architecture diagrams, IAM policy documentation, encryption posture summary, and incident response runbook excerpts
  • Coordinated directly with their PDM to flag the resubmission and signal renewed partner investment
  • Managed the Partner Central submission end-to-end and responded to one minor clarification request within 48 hours
Outcomes
  • FTR approved on first Grnmrk-managed submission
  • AWS Qualified Software badge awarded
  • SaaS Co-sell Benefit enrollment completed within 4 weeks of FTR approval
  • $85K in MAP funding unlocked and applied to two active migration deals
  • PDM relationship re-engaged with monthly co-sell cadence reinstated
  • Three co-sell opportunities submitted to ACE within 60 days of enrollment

"We'd burned almost a year on this. Grnmrk came in, figured out exactly what we'd been doing wrong, and got it done in five weeks. The FTR was just the beginning."

VP of Partnerships, Series B Security ISV

The Situation
Funding AWS Wanted to Give Them. Unclaimed.

This DevOps platform had been an AWS Select-tier partner for over two years. Their product had a clear migration use case: customers regularly used it to modernize CI/CD pipelines during AWS migrations, and their PDM had mentioned MAP funding multiple times in passing. But nobody had ever followed up, and the team did not have the bandwidth to figure out how the program worked.

When we audited their closed deals from the prior two years, we identified eight engagements that had been MAP-eligible and none had been submitted. The total eligible funding window across those deals was approximately $120K.

What We Did
  • Conducted a retroactive deal audit across 24 months of closed business to identify MAP-eligible engagements
  • Classified each deal by MAP tier (Lite, Standard, Large) based on migration scope and AWS consumption data
  • Built MAP SPI submissions for the 8 qualifying opportunities, working with their delivery team to assemble required migration artifacts
  • Established a forward-looking MAP qualification process so their AEs could identify MAP-eligible deals at discovery, not after close
  • Created a funding tracking dashboard and intake process so no eligible deal would be missed going forward
  • Coordinated with PDM on SPI approvals and ensured each submission was linked to active ACE pipeline
Outcomes
  • $120K in MAP funding approved across 8 submitted SPIs
  • MAP qualification process embedded into AE deal qualification workflow
  • Funding tracking dashboard deployed, updated monthly as managed services client
  • Three additional MAP opportunities in active pipeline at engagement close
  • PDM co-sponsored two of the largest SPIs, strengthening the field relationship

"We knew MAP existed. We just never had the bandwidth to figure it out. Grnmrk turned what was already in our deal history into real money, then built the process so we'd never miss it again."

The Situation
Enrolled. Co-Sell Going Nowhere.

This compliance automation ISV had done the right things early: they had passed FTR, enrolled in the SaaS Co-sell Benefit, and had a legitimate product that AWS customers were buying. But their co-sell motion was flat. They had an ACE pipeline of zero. Their PDM had gone silent. And their AEs had no idea how to involve AWS in a deal.

The problem was not awareness. They knew what co-sell was supposed to look like. The problem was that they had no repeatable process for making it happen. There was no ACE submission standard, no PDM engagement cadence, no field rep relationship outside of their single PDM contact, and no internal enablement for their sales team.

What We Did
  • Designed a co-sell motion from scratch: ACE submission criteria, opportunity qualification threshold, and PDM engagement protocol
  • Ran a 2-hour AE enablement session on AWS co-sell mechanics: how to identify co-sell opportunities, how to frame them for AWS, and what to expect from the process
  • Reactivated the PDM relationship with a structured re-engagement: shared updated product positioning, three qualified pipeline accounts, and a proposed 30-day co-sell pilot
  • Facilitated introductions to 3 AWS field reps in their top territories through PDM and PSM network
  • Submitted 8 initial ACE opportunities from existing pipeline to establish baseline and signal investment
  • Built a weekly co-sell cadence: ACE hygiene review, PDM sync, and new opportunity qualification, all managed by Grnmrk on a fractional basis
Outcomes
  • 11 net-new AWS-sourced opportunities created in the first 90 days
  • $1.4M in co-sell pipeline generated, 4 opportunities at late stage
  • 3 AWS field reps actively routing deals with cadenced monthly touchpoints established
  • AE team completing ACE submissions independently within 60 days
  • PDM relationship re-established with bi-weekly sync cadence in place
  • Co-sell playbook documented and delivered for internal use

"We had the SaaS Co-sell Benefit and nothing to show for it. Grnmrk built the motion that made it real — and did it in a way our AEs could actually own after they left."

Listed. Invisible. Earning Nothing.

This FinTech SaaS platform had invested real effort in getting their AWS Marketplace listing live. They had the badge, the listing page, the product description. Nine months later: zero transactions. Their listing existed, but it was not connected to anything: no private offer strategy, no co-sell alignment, no CPPO motion with channel partners who were already selling their product.

The listing had been built as a compliance checkbox, not a revenue channel. Nobody had thought through how a buyer would actually transact through it or which deals should go through Marketplace versus direct.

What We Did
  • Audited the existing listing: identified gaps in product description, pricing structure, category selection, and search keyword coverage
  • Rebuilt the listing copy and category tags to align with how AWS buyers actually search for compliance and financial risk management tools
  • Designed a private offer strategy: tiered pricing for direct enterprise buyers, and a CPPO template for their 3 existing channel partners
  • Identified 6 deals in late-stage pipeline where Marketplace transacting would reduce procurement friction and help close faster
  • Structured the first private offer and managed the AWS Marketplace deal room process end-to-end for the largest account
  • Onboarded all 3 channel partners to CPPO and built partner-specific offer templates and trained their account teams on the transaction flow

They came to Grnmrk not because they were doing everything wrong, but because they needed someone to own the AWS program so their alliances manager could focus on the partnerships where she added the most strategic value.
